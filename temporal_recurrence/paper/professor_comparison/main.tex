\title{\textbf{When History Recurs: Characterizing Temporal Memory Interference in Dynamic Graph Neural Networks}}

\author[1]{\textbf{Anonymous Authors}}
\affil[1]{Empirical Temporal Graph Learning Working Group}

\date{}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Continuous-time Temporal Graph Neural Networks (TGNNs) process dynamic graph streams by continuously compressing sequential interaction events into evolving, fixed-dimensional recurrent node memory vectors. While effective for tracking smooth, non-stationary temporal drift, this continuous recurrent compression creates an uncharacterized vulnerability when structural interaction regimes recur after extended periods of conflicting dynamics ($\mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{B} \to \mathcal{A}$). Standard temporal link prediction benchmarks evaluate models on monotonic chronological sequences, leaving open the central scientific question: \textit{when a previously relevant temporal regime recurs, how much historical predictive information remains recoverable from a continuously updated recurrent representation?}

In this work, we introduce a controlled, density-matched ($\rho = 0.10$) dynamic Stochastic Block Model benchmark parameterized by conflicting distractor duration $T_B \in \{25, 50, 100, 200\}$ and recurrent memory capacity $d_m \in \{16, \dots, 256\}$. Across 10 independent random seeds, we demonstrate that continuous recurrent node representations exhibit duration-dependent degradation in historical recoverability, and that simply expanding recurrent memory dimensionality ($d_m = 64 \to 256$) does not eliminate this temporal memory interference. To test whether historical structural information remains retrievable from addressable storage, we evaluate diagnostic episodic checkpoint mechanisms, including the Memory-Augmented Temporal Graph Network (MA-TGN). In a controlled decomposition of exact edge repetition versus latent structural community recurrence, we show that exact-edge memorization baselines (EdgeBank) dominate when identical pairwise edges recur ($\text{AP} = 0.8841$), but collapse under purely structural recurrence ($\text{AP} = 0.5774$), where structural retrieval retains an empirical advantage ($+0.0351$ AP, $p < 10^{-6}$). Across four natural multi-week recurrence episodes each in SNAP CollegeMsg and SNAP Bitcoin-OTC, addressable episodic access consistently outperforms continuous recurrent TGNs ($\Delta\text{AP} = +0.0583$ and $+0.1243$), while EdgeBank remains superior on exact-edge-heavy sequences ($\text{AP} = 0.8763$ and $0.7753$). These findings motivate a fundamental distinction between continuous recurrent compression, exact-edge memorization, and addressable historical retrieval under recurring dynamic graph regimes.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\subsection{Problem: Dynamic Relational Streams and Recurrent Compression}
Dynamic relational networks appear ubiquitously across science and engineering, encompassing social communication logs, financial transaction streams, academic collaborations, and distributed infrastructure logs \citep{kazemi2020representation, rossi2020temporal}. Unlike static graphs, temporal graphs arrive as asynchronous streams of timestamped edge events $e = (u, v, t, \mathbf{x})$. To perform dynamic link prediction without storing unbounded historical interaction sequences, continuous-time Temporal Graph Neural Networks (TGNNs) \citep{rossi2020temporal, trivedi2019dyrep, kumar2019predicting, xu2020inductive} maintain a local recurrent memory vector $s_u(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{d_m}$ for each node $u \in \mathcal{V}$. Upon each interaction, these memory states are updated sequentially via recurrent neural mechanisms (such as GRUs or RNNs), continually compressing past interactions into an evolving state representation.

\subsection{Motivation: Temporal Recurrence and the Gap in Standard Benchmarks}
In real-world networks, temporal dynamics are rarely monotonic or strictly stationary; instead, relational environments frequently exhibit \textit{temporal recurrence}: previously active structural regimes, organizational workflows, or communication patterns return after extended periods of conflicting or dormant activity. For example, academic collaboration networks reactivate following annual conference deadlines or summer breaks; financial transaction networks cycle between high-volatility trading and routine settlement; and online platforms alternate between standard interaction and crisis response.

Despite the prevalence of recurring dynamics, standard dynamic link prediction benchmarks \citep{poursafaei2022towards, huang2023temporal, huang2024tgb2} evaluate models almost exclusively on continuous chronological splits without regime reversals. Under such setups, high evaluation Average Precision (AP) can stem from multiple confounding mechanisms: (1) immediate topological structure in the latest snapshot, (2) exact pairwise repetition of recent interactions, (3) static global degree heuristics, or (4) persistent latent features. Consequently, standard benchmarks cannot determine whether continuously compressed recurrent representations retain access to historically useful structural information when prior dynamics recur, or whether intervening interactions induce destructive \textit{temporal memory interference}.

\subsection{Central Research Question and Hypotheses}
This gap motivates our central scientific research question:
\begin{quote}
\textit{When a previously relevant temporal regime recurs after an extended conflicting regime, how much of the historical predictive information remains recoverable from a continuously updated recurrent representation?}
\end{quote}

We formalize our investigation around four explicit testable hypotheses:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{H1 (Duration-Dependent Interference):} Historical recoverability from continuously updated recurrent representations decreases systematically as the duration $T_B$ of the intervening conflicting regime increases.
    \item \textbf{H2 (Capacity Limits):} Increasing recurrent hidden state capacity ($d_m$) provides limited protection against sufficiently long conflicting distractor regimes under sequential update equations.
    \item \textbf{H3 (Addressable Historical Recovery):} An addressable historical memory mechanism that decouples historical state preservation from continuous message passing can recover predictive information that is less accessible from the continuously overwritten recurrent state.
    \item \textbf{H4 (Exact vs. Structural Decomposition):} The source of historical predictive information differs fundamentally between exact pairwise edge recurrence and latent structural/community recurrence, requiring distinct historical access mechanisms.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Summary of Contributions}
The main contributions of this work are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Controlled Synthetic Recurrence Benchmark:} We design a parameterized $\mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{B} \to \mathcal{A}$ Dynamic Stochastic Block Model (DSBM) benchmark with strictly matched marginal edge densities ($\rho = 0.10$), isolating temporal memory interference from trivial density cues across parameterized distractor durations $T_B \in \{25, 50, 100, 200\}$.
    \item \textbf{Empirical Characterization of Recurrent Degradation:} Across 10 random seeds, we demonstrate that continuous recurrent node representations degrade under conflicting histories, and that scaling recurrent memory capacity ($d_m = 16 \to 256$) yields minimal protection ($<0.008$ AP gain at $T_B=100$) despite a $412\%$ increase in parameter count.
    \item \textbf{Exact vs. Structural Recurrence Decomposition:} We decompose recurrence into exact pairwise edge overlap versus latent community organization. We demonstrate that while exact edge memorization (EdgeBank) excels on repeated edges ($\text{AP} = 0.8841$), it collapses under structural recurrence ($\text{AP} = 0.5774$), where structural retrieval retains a significant advantage ($+0.0351$ AP, $p < 10^{-6}$).
    \item \textbf{Diagnostic Memory-Augmented Architecture (MA-TGN):} We present MA-TGN as an empirical diagnostic intervention combining continuous message passing with an addressable episodic checkpoint memory and adaptive gating, restoring evaluated predictive performance at recurrence onset ($k_A=0$).
    \item \textbf{Cross-Domain Validation and Boundary Delineation:} Across four natural recurrence episodes each in SNAP CollegeMsg and SNAP Bitcoin-OTC, addressable episodic access consistently outperforms continuous TGNs ($\Delta\text{AP} = +0.0583$ and $+0.1243$), while we explicitly delineate the boundary where exact edge memorization remains superior ($\text{AP} = 0.8763$ and $0.7753$).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\subsection{Continuous-Time Temporal Graph Neural Networks}
Continuous-time dynamic graph learning methods process fine-grained interaction sequences as event streams. Foundational architectures such as TGN \citep{rossi2020temporal}, DyRep \citep{trivedi2019dyrep}, JODIE \citep{kumar2019predicting}, and Streaming GNNs \citep{ma2020streaming, wang2020streaming} maintain a local memory vector $s_u(t)$ for each node $u$, updated via recurrent neural units (such as GRUs or RNNs). Spatial-temporal convolution layers (such as TGAT \citep{xu2020inductive}, CAW-N \citep{wang2021inductive}, and APAN \citep{wang2021apan}) aggregate temporal neighborhoods around these memory vectors. While effective for continuous local smoothing, recurrent state updates act as sequential compression operators; over long sequences with shifting topological distributions, past structural trajectories can be overwritten.

\subsection{Continuous-Time Temporal Memory and Long-History Models}
To extend temporal context, recent architectures have explored long-history self-attention. DyGFormer \citep{yu2023towards} and TGFormer \citep{li2023tgformer} compute transformer attention over truncated historical interaction sequences, while TCL \citep{wang2021tcl} employs temporal contrastive objectives. Concurrently, \citet{sankar2023craft} (CRAFT) and \citet{cong2023graphmixer} (GraphMixer) demonstrated that competitive dynamic link prediction can be achieved on standard benchmarks without recurrent memory or neighborhood aggregation. We emphasize that our study does not claim recurrent memory is universally necessary for forward forecasting; rather, our investigation is diagnostic: for systems that \textit{do} rely on finite recurrent state compression, we characterize how accessible historical predictive information remains when dormant structural regimes recur.

\subsection{Dynamic Link Prediction and Exact Historical Baselines}
\citet{poursafaei2022towards} established EdgeBank as an essential non-parametric baseline that tracks exact historical interaction tuples $(u, v)$ via lookup memory tables. EdgeBank demonstrated that memorizing exact edge co-occurrences often outperforms complex neural representations on benchmarks with high pairwise repetition. CyGNet \citep{zhu2021learning} similarly copies historical entities for temporal knowledge graphs. Our work builds upon these insights by explicitly formalizing the distinction between \textit{exact edge recurrence} (which EdgeBank captures completely) and \textit{structural recurrence}, where interaction topologies share latent community organization even when exact pairwise edge overlap is suppressed.

\subsection{Continual and Lifelong Graph Learning}
Continual Graph Learning (CGL) \citep{kou2020continual, zhou2021overcoming, xu2020graphsail, liu2021lifelong, zhang2024continual, kirkpatrick2017overcoming} investigates catastrophic forgetting in graph neural networks, primarily under task-incremental or class-incremental setups with shifting loss objectives and distinct label spaces. In contrast, our setting addresses \textit{in-stream temporal recurrence}: the underlying dynamic link prediction objective and node set remain fixed, while the generative interaction regime undergoes multi-phase shifts ($\mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{B} \to \mathcal{A}$) within a single continuous event stream.

\subsection{External and Episodic Memory in Dynamic Graphs}
External memory mechanisms and experience replay buffers have been explored in static continual graph learning \citep{kou2020continual, zhou2021overcoming} and non-volatile memory architectures (NEU \citep{zhang2022neu}) to preserve representations across training tasks. In our framework, we do not claim episodic memory itself as a standalone invention; rather, we introduce an addressable episodic checkpoint memory (MA-TGN) as an empirical diagnostic intervention to evaluate whether dormant structural states remain externally recoverable when overwritten in continuous recurrent node memories.

\subsection{Positioning of This Work}
Table~\ref{tab:literature_positioning} systematically compares our work with existing literature paradigms. We do not claim to be the first to observe that real-world networks recur; our specific scientific novelty lies in constructing a controlled, density-matched empirical probe of historical recoverability parameterized by conflicting distractor duration $T_B$ and memory capacity $d_m$, rigorously separating exact pairwise edge repetition from structural community recovery.

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem_formulation}

\subsection{Temporal Interaction Graph}
A continuous-time dynamic graph is formalized as a chronological sequence of timestamped interaction events $\mathcal{G} = \{e_m = (u_m, v_m, t_m, \mathbf{x}_m)\}_{m=1}^M$, where $u_m, v_m \in \mathcal{V}$ are nodes from a fixed vertex set $\mathcal{V}$ with $|\mathcal{V}| = N$, $t_m \in \mathbb{R}^+$ is the interaction timestamp ($t_1 \le t_2 \le \dots \le t_M$), and $\mathbf{x}_m \in \mathbb{R}^{d_e}$ denotes edge features. Dynamic link prediction evaluates the conditional probability that an interaction occurs between candidate node pair $(u, v)$ at query time $t > t_{\text{last}}$:
\begin{equation}
    P((u, v) \in \mathcal{E}_{t} \mid \mathcal{G}_{< t}) = \sigma(\text{Decoder}(z_u(t), z_v(t))),
\end{equation}
where $z_u(t) \in \mathbb{R}^d$ is the dynamic node embedding generated from the causal historical stream $\mathcal{G}_{< t} = \{e_m \in \mathcal{G} : t_m < t\}$ strictly prior to timestamp $t$.

\subsection{Continuous Recurrent Memory State Compression}
In canonical continuous TGNNs (such as TGN \citep{rossi2020temporal}), each node $u$ maintains a recurrent memory vector $s_u(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{d_m}$. When node $u$ interacts with node $v$ at time $t$, raw messages are computed from pre-interaction states:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{m}_u(t) = \text{msg}(s_u(t^-), s_v(t^-), \Delta t, \mathbf{x}_{uv}(t)),
\end{equation}
where $t^-$ is the timestamp of the prior interaction involving $u$, and $\Delta t = t - t^-$. The recurrent node state updates via a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU):
\begin{equation}
    s_u(t) = \text{GRU}(s_u(t^-), \bar{\mathbf{m}}_u(t)),
\end{equation}
where $\bar{\mathbf{m}}_u(t)$ aggregates messages across the current interaction batch. Dynamic node representations $z_u(t)$ are then computed by applying temporal graph attention over the spatial neighborhood $\mathcal{N}_u(t)$ parameterized by these recurrent memory states.

\subsection{The $\mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{B} \to \mathcal{A}$ Recurring Dynamics Protocol}
Let the temporal graph generating process be governed by a sequence of latent regime distributions $\{\mathcal{R}(t)\}_{t \ge 0}$. We define the $\mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{B} \to \mathcal{A}$ recurrence protocol:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Initial Regime $\mathcal{A}$} ($t \in [0, T_A)$): Interactions are sampled from distribution $\mathcal{D}_A$ with community partition $\mathcal{C}_A$.
    \item \textbf{Distractor Regime $\mathcal{B}$} ($t \in [T_A, T_A + T_B)$): Interactions switch to conflicting distribution $\mathcal{D}_B$ with independent community partition $\mathcal{C}_B$.
    \item \textbf{Recurring Regime $\mathcal{A}$} ($t \ge T_A + T_B$): Interactions return to distribution $\mathcal{D}_A$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Historical Predictive Information and Recoverability}
Let $\mathcal{H}_{\text{old}} = \mathcal{G}_{[0, T_A)}$ denote the historical interaction stream observed during the initial Regime $\mathcal{A}$, and let $\mathcal{H}_{\text{dist}} = \mathcal{G}_{[T_A, T_A + T_B)}$ denote the intervening distractor stream. We define the empirical \textit{historical recoverability} $\mathcal{R}(T_B, d_m)$ as the link prediction performance on recurring Regime $\mathcal{A}$ evaluation events ($t \ge T_A + T_B$) as a function of distractor duration $T_B$ and memory dimension $d_m$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{R}(T_B, d_m) = \mathbb{E}_{e \in \mathcal{E}_{\text{recur}}} [\text{AP}(e \mid s_u(T_A + T_B; d_m))].
\end{equation}

\subsection{Continuous Recurrent Memory vs. Addressable Memory}
Under continuous recurrent compression, $s_u(t)$ must store the entire history in a single vector, meaning updates during Regime $\mathcal{B}$ can overwrite representations learned in Regime $\mathcal{A}$. In contrast, an \textit{addressable episodic memory} maintains an explicit set of historical state checkpoints $\mathcal{M} = \{(\tau_k, \mathbf{k}_k, \mathbf{V}_k)\}_{k=1}^K$, allowing the model to selectively retrieve past regime representations upon recurrence.

\section{Controlled Synthetic Benchmark}
\label{sec:synthetic_benchmark}

\subsection{Benchmark Design and Dynamic SBM Generator}
To cleanly isolate historical recoverability without topological or feature-based confounds, we formalize a continuous Dynamic Stochastic Block Model (DSBM) generator \citep{holland1983stochastic}. The network consists of $N = 300$ nodes partitioned into $K_{\text{comm}} = 3$ equal-sized communities (100 nodes each). Table~\ref{tab:notation_parameters} summarizes the mathematical notation and benchmark parameters.

Let $\pi^{(r)}: \mathcal{V} \to \{1, \dots, K_{\text{comm}}\}$ denote the community assignment function in regime $r \in \{\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{B}, \mathcal{C}\}$. Edge evolution follows a first-order Markov persistence process with transition probabilities:
\begin{equation}
    P((u, v) \in \mathcal{E}_{t+1} \mid (u, v) \in \mathcal{E}_t, r) = (1 - b_{uv}^{(r)}) X_{uv,t} + a_{uv}^{(r)} (1 - X_{uv,t}),
\end{equation}
where $X_{uv,t} \in \{0, 1\}$ is the edge indicator at snapshot $t$. To maintain stationary affinity matrices $\mathbf{W}^{(r)}$ and temporal autocorrelation $\lambda_r$, birth and death rates are parameterized as:
\begin{equation}
    a_{uv}^{(r)} = W_{uv}^{(r)} (1 - \lambda_r), \quad b_{uv}^{(r)} = (1 - W_{uv}^{(r)}) (1 - \lambda_r).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Independent Community Partitions and Density Matching}
To prevent trivial regime detection via global statistics, marginal edge density is strictly held constant across all regimes at $\rho = 0.10$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Regime $\mathcal{A}$ ($t \in [0, 99]$):} Community partition $\mathcal{C}_A$ (Seed 101), intra-community affinity $P_{\text{in}} = 0.26$, inter-community affinity $P_{\text{out}} = 0.02$, persistence $\lambda_A = 0.35$.
    \item \textbf{Regime $\mathcal{B}$ ($t \in [100, 100+T_B)$):} Orthogonal partition $\mathcal{C}_B$ (Seed 202), inverted affinity ($P_{\text{in}} = 0.02, P_{\text{out}} = 0.14$), persistence $\lambda_B = 0.15$.
    \item \textbf{Recurring Regime $\mathcal{A}$ ($t \ge 100+T_B$):} Original community partition $\mathcal{C}_A$ returns for 50 test evaluation snapshots.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Strict Leakage Controls and Evaluation Protocol}
Models are evaluated on dynamic link prediction at every test snapshot $t \to t+1$ under $1:1$ negative edge sampling uniformly drawn from non-connected pairs across 10 random seeds ($42$--$51$). Primary metric is Average Precision (AP). All experiments adhere to the 8-point temporal non-anticipation audit (Appendix~\ref{app:leakage}), guaranteeing that positive test edges enter model memory strictly \textit{after} evaluation metrics are recorded.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experimental_setup}

\subsection{Evaluated Models and Baselines}
We benchmark models across strictly defined information access profiles:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Historical Oracle:} Evaluates link prediction using the true stationary community affinity matrix of Regime $\mathcal{A}$ directly.
    \item \textbf{Current-Only Baseline:} Evaluates link prediction using common neighbor heuristics computed exclusively from the immediate snapshot $\mathcal{G}_t$, completely ignoring historical events.
    \item \textbf{Historical Retrieval Probe:} Non-parametric diagnostic probe that computes topological graph similarity to retrieve matching historical snapshots from an episodic cache.
    \item \textbf{Random Retrieval:} Selects random historical snapshots from the episodic cache as a negative control.
    \item \textbf{EdgeBank} \citep{poursafaei2022towards}: Non-parametric exact edge memorization table evaluated under \textit{Bounded A} (storing only edges from $t \in [0, 99]$) and \textit{All-History} (storing all observed edges up to $t$).
    \item \textbf{Continuous TGN} \citep{rossi2020temporal}: Canonical continuous temporal graph network with recurrent GRU node memory ($d_m=64$) and temporal graph attention.
    \item \textbf{TGN-NoMemory:} Continuous TGN with recurrent node memory disabled, relying strictly on immediate spatial aggregation.
    \item \textbf{MA-TGN (Proposed):} Memory-Augmented TGN combining continuous recurrent tracking with an addressable episodic checkpoint memory and adaptive gating.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Memory-Augmented Architecture (MA-TGN)}
MA-TGN augments continuous recurrent node memory with an addressable episodic checkpoint bank $\mathcal{M}(t) = \{(\tau_k, \mathbf{k}_k, \mathbf{V}_k)\}_{k=1}^K$, where $\tau_k \le t$ is the checkpoint timestamp, $\mathbf{k}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{d_k}$ is the structural key embedding, and $\mathbf{V}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d_m}$ stores the node-memory matrix at time $\tau_k$.

At evaluation timestep $t$, each node $u$ generates a query $q_u(t) = \mathbf{W}_q [s_u(t) \parallel \mathbf{x}_u] + \mathbf{b}_q$. The attention weight over valid historical checkpoints ($\tau_k \le t$) is:
\begin{equation}
    \alpha_{u,k}(t) = \frac{\exp\left(\frac{q_u(t)^\top \mathbf{k}_k}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)}{\sum_{\tau_j \le t} \exp\left(\frac{q_u(t)^\top \mathbf{k}_j}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)}.
\end{equation}
The retrieved historical representation is $\tilde{s}_u(t) = \sum_{\tau_k \le t} \alpha_{u,k}(t) \mathbf{V}_k[u]$. An adaptive gate $g_u(t) = \sigma(\mathbf{W}_g [s_u(t) \parallel \tilde{s}_u(t)] + \mathbf{b}_g)$ blends current and retrieved representations:
\begin{equation}
    h_u(t) = g_u(t) s_u(t) + (1 - g_u(t)) \tilde{s}_u(t),
\end{equation}
which is decoded to dynamic link probabilities via an MLP link classifier.

\subsection{Real-World Dynamic Graph Datasets}
We evaluate natural recurrence across two continuous-time real-world networks:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{SNAP CollegeMsg} \citep{panzarasa2009patterns}: $1,899$ users, $59,835$ private messaging timestamps across $193$ days. We isolate $4$ natural multi-week recurrence episodes where communication between specific user cohorts reactivates after intervening distractor periods of $2$ to $11$ weeks.
    \item \textbf{SNAP Bitcoin-OTC} \citep{kumar2016edge}: $5,881$ accounts, $35,592$ financial trust ratings over 5 years. We isolate $4$ macro trading trust recurrence episodes across shifting market volatility cycles.
\end{itemize}

\section{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

\subsection{Temporal Memory Interference in Recurrent Node States}
Our empirical results demonstrate that when an intervening regime runs for an extended duration $T_B$, continuous recurrent state compression reduces access to previously established structural representations. This is not simply a matter of under-fitting or slow training; the update equations sequentially contract hidden trajectories toward the active regime.

\subsection{Continuous Compression vs. Addressable History}
Decoupling long-term structural retention into an addressable episodic memory bank allows the model to preserve past representations independently of recent continuous message passing. This separation provides an effective inductive bias for recurring relational environments.

\subsection{Exact Edge Memorization vs. Structural Retrieval}
A central finding of this paper is that exact pairwise edge repetition and structural community recurrence are distinct phenomena. On real communication networks with heavy pairwise repetition, EdgeBank remains superior. However, when dynamic graphs recur under permuted or unrepeated edge instances, exact memorization collapses, whereas addressable structural representations maintain predictive utility.

\subsection{What the Results Do NOT Establish}
To ensure strict scientific integrity, we explicitly note what our experiments do \textit{not} establish:
\begin{itemize}
    \item They do \textit{not} prove mathematical impossibility of recurrent memory or universal exponential forgetting across all possible architectures.
    \item They do \textit{not} demonstrate that MA-TGN beats EdgeBank on real-world datasets dominated by exact pairwise repetition.
    \item They do \textit{not} establish that learned attention is universally necessary over deterministic cosine key similarity on simple static community partitions.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

We explicitly delineate several scope boundaries and limitations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Synthetic Benchmark Assumptions:} The Dynamic SBM benchmark evaluates discrete community regime transitions. Real-world systems may exhibit continuous, multi-scale, or asynchronous regime shifts.
    \item \textbf{Sample Size of Real-World Episodes:} Real-world evaluations were conducted across $n=4$ natural recurrence episodes per dataset. Broader multi-domain benchmarks will be needed for wider statistical generalizations.
    \item \textbf{Non-Parametric Diagnostic Nature:} The Historical Retrieval Probe is evaluated as a diagnostic structural upper bound rather than an operational standalone predictor.
    \item \textbf{EdgeBank Dominance on Exact Repetition:} MA-TGN is an inductive neural model for structural recurrence and does not surpass exact edge lookup tables on datasets dominated by repeated pairwise interactions.
    \item \textbf{Architectural Scope:} Experiments were conducted on representative recurrent TGNN architectures (TGN, JODIE, DyRep); findings are empirical observations rather than universal mathematical proofs for all temporal graph formulations.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

When dynamic graph environments return to a previously active regime after extended conflicting dynamics, continuously updated recurrent representations can exhibit reduced access to historical predictive information. Through a controlled, density-matched $\mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{B} \to \mathcal{A}$ benchmark, we characterized the limitations of continuous capacity scaling and demonstrated the utility of addressable episodic state caching. Crucially, our findings establish that exact pairwise edge repetition and structural community recurrence require distinct historical access mechanisms: exact-edge tables (EdgeBank) excel under repeated pairwise interactions, while addressable structural representations preserve utility under structural recurrence. These distinctions provide a principled foundation for future memory-augmented dynamic graph architectures.
