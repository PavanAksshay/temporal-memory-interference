\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

When a previously relevant temporal regime recurs after prolonged conflicting dynamics, continuously updated recurrent representations can exhibit reduced access to historically predictive information. In this work, we formalized this phenomenon through a parameterized $\mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{B} \to \mathcal{A}$ benchmark and characterized the limitations of continuous capacity scaling. Addressable episodic memory (MA-TGN) provides an effective architectural mechanism for historical access, restoring evaluated predictive performance at recurrence onset ($k_A=0$) and improving over continuous TGNs across evaluated real-world recurrence episodes in SNAP CollegeMsg and SNAP Bitcoin-OTC. At the same time, exact-edge memorization (EdgeBank) remains substantially stronger when recurrence is dominated by repeated identical node pairs. These results demonstrate that temporal recurrence is not a single uniform memory problem: exact edge repetition and structural community recurrence impose distinct demands on historical graph representation.
